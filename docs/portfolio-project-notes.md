3. HIKEBUDDY

Current role / context:
- Role: HCI Researcher
- Team members: Saanika Medishetty, Elyssa Abbott
- Professor/Advisor: Austin Wright

Current overview:
Designed HikeBuddy, a hands-free auditory navigation system for hiking intended to reduce visual distraction by delivering clear voice-based directions during outdoor walks.

Users:
- Casual hikers
- Students on short hikes or outdoor walks

Problem:
- Hiking navigation often requires repeatedly looking at a phone.
- Visual attention to the screen can be inconvenient or unsafe on uneven terrain.
- Existing navigation tools are visually dominant rather than optimized for hands-free, in-motion use.

Known contributions:
- Contributed to interaction design for an auditory user interface.
- Designed and prototyped the mobile interface in Figma.
- Helped define voice interactions such as “repeat” and “next.”
- Participated in creating a Wizard of Oz auditory prototype.
- Assisted with observational user studies.
- Took notes and helped synthesize findings.
- Explored cognitive load and hands-free interaction.

Prototype approach:
- Low-fidelity Wizard of Oz auditory prototype.
- One team member manually delivered spoken navigation instructions.
- Another updated screens in Figma to simulate responses.
- Study took place during real hikes on Cerro San Luis Obispo Trail.

Study:
- Three participants.
- Approximately 1 hour 15 minutes per participant.
- Participants completed a hike with the prototype.
- Users could request repetitions and interact with the navigation flow.

Findings:
- Users quickly understood the audio-navigation concept.
- Hands-free interaction felt natural.
- Voice commands were easy to remember.
- Directional ambiguity was a problem where multiple paths existed.
- Some users wanted more reassurance/context.
- Participants differed in how much feedback they preferred.
- Some relied on audio, while others still looked at the screen.
- Mental model was generally “spoken GPS.”
- Lower-confidence navigators appeared to want more confirmation.

Future design ideas:
- Periodic confirmation messages such as “You’re on the right track.”
- Landmark-based directions such as “Turn left near the bench.”
- Future experiment comparing minimal feedback vs. confirmatory feedback.
- Could measure confidence, navigation errors, perceived workload, etc.

Skills / tools:
- Figma
- UX/UI design
- Auditory interface design
- Interaction design
- Wizard of Oz prototyping
- Usability testing
- Observational research

Recommended portfolio positioning:
FEATURED.
Strongest project for UX research / HCI.

Why it is strong:
- Shows research thinking rather than only interface design.
- Demonstrates prototyping under technical constraints.
- Shows real-world field evaluation.
- Shows mental models, behavioral differences, synthesis, and future experiment design.
- Adds range to the portfolio.

Best story structure:
Research question -> design requirements -> prototype strategy -> field study -> observations -> synthesized insights -> design implications -> future experiment

Strong opportunity to highlight:
- The Wizard of Oz method is a great artifact because it shows how you tested an interaction before fully building it.
- The difference between users who wanted minimal interruption and users who wanted reassurance is a strong HCI insight.
- The project can show how research changed the next design/research direction.
- Emphasize what YOU specifically designed, observed, or synthesized in the team.

Current gaps / information that would strengthen it:

- Which parts of the Figma interface did you personally design?
- Which parts of the audio interaction did you personally define?
- Did you personally moderate any sessions?
- Did you personally operate the Wizard of Oz prototype?

## Biggest Opportunities Across The Whole Portfolio

1. SHOW DECISIONS, NOT ONLY SCREENS
Instead of only showing finished Figma screens, pair them with:
- what changed
- why it changed
- what evidence influenced the decision

2. MAKE INDIVIDUAL OWNERSHIP CLEAR
Several projects are team projects. Every page should clearly distinguish:
- what the team did
- what Cory personally owned

3. CONNECT DESIGN TO ENGINEERING
This is one of the strongest portfolio differentiators.
Where possible, show:
- Figma
- implementation
- technical constraints
- how design changed during development

4. USE RESEARCH EVIDENCE
For projects with interviews or usability testing, include:
- observation
- finding
- implication
- resulting design change

5. AVOID OVER-CLAIMING OUTCOMES
If there are no metrics, do not invent them.
It is completely acceptable to say:
- prototype tested with X participants
- stakeholder feedback informed Y
- designed for a particular outcome
- next step would be validation / measurement

6. LET PROJECTS HAVE DIFFERENT SHAPES
Farm-ng should feel like a product + engineering story.
Athleat should feel like an end-to-end product design story.
HikeBuddy should feel like a research study.
Cáritas should feel like a stakeholder/workflow design story.
Photobooth should feel like a build/design mini-project.
Pivot should feel like a rapid design sprint.
